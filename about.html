The Designer

Behind
Pretty Absurd.

An independent designer making clothes that look impossible in photos and make complete sense in person.

Kaylah, Designer & Founder
The Story

Clothes that belong in a film you can't quite place.

Kaylah started Pretty Absurd with a simple bet: build three dresses no one could ignore, then see who's brave enough to wear them out.

All three share the same bones: a corset bodice, a sweetheart neckline, a pleated skirt. From there, they split hard. Pink Satin is a Barbie dream sequence. Ice Blue would not look out of place on a Paris catwalk, sharper and colder than the pink. The Polka Dot drags in a bit of Marilyn Monroe, thick black dots on white Duchess Satin, the kind of print that reads from across a room.

The name is the whole pitch. Pretty Absurd makes clothes that don't belong anywhere in particular, and wears that on purpose. People stare because the pieces are correct in ways that shouldn't work.

The Philosophy
"Pretty Absurd is built on one idea: clothes should make you feel like the most interesting person in the room. Not the most tasteful. The most interesting."

Kaylah doesn't buy the idea that clothes have to pick a side, feminine or serious, playful or polished. The corset shaping holds real structure. The polka dots don't apologize for being loud. Both live in the same dress, on purpose.

The Process

Kaylah places every seam on purpose.
The mess shows up when you wear it. Not before.

01

Structure first

Every dress starts with the shape. The corset silhouette is drafted before a single piece of Duchess Satin is cut.

02

Fabric as character

Fabric carries most of the personality here. Duchess Satin catches light in a way that shifts depending on how you move. Polka dots do the talking before you open your mouth.

03

Details that earn their place

A ruffle finishes the sweetheart neckline, lacing runs up the back, and the zip stays invisible at the side.
